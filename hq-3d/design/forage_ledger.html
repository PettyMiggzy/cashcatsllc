Answer first

You cannot max all three in a week — and you shouldn’t want to

The spec asks for two things that pull against each other: Energy that stops players blitzing levels, and a full clear in seven days. Priced the obvious way — a curve of 40 × L1.55 — maxing one skill takes 575 days of two-hour sessions, and a week gets you 1.22% of the way.

So the curve is 8 × L1.30 + 30. Mastery is a season-long goal rather than a weekend or a myth, and a first week is a real chunk of the tree instead of a rounding error. If Ahmad genuinely wants seven days, the Energy gate has to go — and then the content is finished in a week.

What a week actually buys

Seven days, two hours a day

All three skills at once, energy split roughly 72/28 between foraging and brewing, everything not brewed sold raw. Foraging gates the other two — it is the only source of both seeds and brewing stock — which is why it trails them and why it is the skill worth balancing around.

Energy

  • Bar of 120, refilling at 2 per minute — a full bar in an hour, whether you are online or not. That is what makes a two-hour session viable: you spend the bar, log off, and it is full again tomorrow.
  • Two hours a day gives 360 Energy: the 120 banked overnight plus 240 earned while playing.
  • Costs rise faster than value — 2, 4, 9, 18 — so grinding Ultra Rare is a choice with a price, not a free upgrade.
  • Every 25 Forage levels one pick yields more for the same Energy: ×1.00, ×1.25, ×1.50, ×1.75.
  • Harvesting costs no Energy. Its XP is a quarter of foraging’s — otherwise it would be strictly better than the thing that supplies it and nobody would forage twice.
  • Every 10 Harvest levels: faster growth and more per patch. At 100 a patch turns over in a little over half the time and returns roughly three times as much.

Planting. Foraging returns seed or spore 1:1. One seed plants one patch, to a maximum of three patches of the same species. A patch grows one crop and then stops — crops do not stack while you are offline, so each patch gives one banked crop on login plus whatever cycles fit in the session. Modelling it as continuous regrowth had twenty-four common patches yielding 1,080 harvests a day and took Harvest to level 98 inside a week.

Alchemy Mastery

Nineteen brews

Tier gating is the spec’s, unchanged. Recipes for Rare and Ultra Rare must be bought; Common and Uncommon need none. Every potion can be brewed at Alchemy 1 once the recipe is owned — level buys quantity and potency, not access: one more potion per attempt every 10 levels (1 at level 1, 10 at level 100) and +6% potency per milestone. Ingredient lists are built so that all twenty-four species are used — checked, not assumed.

Why one curve

Three separate curves would be three sets of numbers to balance against each other every time a tier price changed. One curve, with the XP per action doing the differentiating, keeps the tuning in one place: foraging pays 10 / 26 / 65 / 170, harvesting a quarter of that, brewing 18 / 45 / 110 / 280.

Milestone levels are highlighted. Foraging pays out at every 25; harvesting and alchemy at every 10, which is why those two feel like they are moving even while foraging is the long grind.

Levels 1–25 are one session

6,150 XP cumulative to reach 25 — about ninety minutes on Rare nodes. That is deliberate: the first milestone has to land inside the first sitting or the system reads as a wall.

Decisions for Ahmad

Four things I had to choose

The week is not achievable

And an achievable one would end the content. I picked six weeks to a maxed forager. Say the word and I’ll move it — it is one coefficient.

One XP curve, not three

XP per action differentiates the skills instead. Cheaper to balance and it keeps the three progress bars legible against each other.

Harvest XP is a quarter of forage

Harvesting costs no Energy. At parity it would dominate and foraging would exist only as a seed tap.

Ultra Rare is six species, not two

Three ultra recipes need twelve ingredients each. With a thin top tier the Gold Shovel becomes a bottleneck rather than a reward.
